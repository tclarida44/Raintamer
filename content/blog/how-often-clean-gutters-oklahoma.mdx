## The short answer

For most Oklahoma City homes, twice a year. Late spring after the pollen and seed drop is done, and late fall after the leaves are down. That cadence handles roughly 90% of homes in the metro.

But that's the average. The right schedule for your home depends on three things: what's overhead, what's on the roof, and what kind of gutter system you already have.

## What's overhead

Walk your property and look up. Then look up again, this time at every season.

If you have mature oaks, expect catkins in spring (those long stringy flowers fall in waves), and acorns plus leaves in fall. Two cleanings, sometimes three.

If you have pine, pine needles fall steadily all year. They mat into the bottom of the gutter and become almost waterproof. Pine homes need either three cleanings a year, or a serious gutter guard.

Cottonwoods drop seed clouds in late spring. The seed itself is light, but it sticks to wet roofs and washes into gutters in clumps. A spring cleaning a couple of weeks after the cotton stops works well.

If your home sits in the open with no overhanging trees, you may genuinely only need an annual inspection. Don't skip it though. Shingle grit, dust, and the occasional stick still find their way in.

## What's on the roof

Asphalt shingles shed a fine grit over their lifetime, especially in the first year or two after a re-roof. That grit ends up in the bottom of the gutter and behaves like wet sand. It doesn't clog, but it adds weight and accelerates corrosion at the seams.

Heavy storms can also strip leaves and small branches off neighboring trees and drop them on your roof. After a major weather event, take a look up before the next rain.

## What kind of gutter system you have

This is where the math changes.

Open gutters with no guard: clean twice a year, three for heavy tree cover.

Gutters with screen-style guards: clean once a year. The screens keep the big stuff out, but fine debris still works through and accumulates underneath.

Gutters with quality stainless micromesh or surface-tension reverse-curve guards: an annual inspection is usually enough. Most years you'll find very little to clean. The point of investing in good guards is exactly this: less time on a ladder, more confidence in the system.

## Signs you're overdue

If you're seeing any of these, schedule a cleaning regardless of the calendar:

- Water spilling over the front of the gutter during rain
- Streaks of dirt down the siding below the gutter
- Plants growing in the gutter (yes, really, it happens)
- Sagging gutters or visible separation from the fascia
- Water pooling at the foundation after a storm

## The case for hiring it out

Plenty of homeowners clean their own gutters. If you're comfortable on a ladder and the work is single-story, it's a reasonable Saturday job.

That said, gutter cleaning is the most common cause of fall-related home injuries in the US. A second-story job, a steep grade around the house, or wet shoes after a storm change the math. Most cleaning companies charge $150 to $300 for a typical home, and the work takes them less than an hour.
